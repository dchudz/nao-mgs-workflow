Protein detection and NR validation: 200 samples

Private experiment • Results recorded 22 October 2026 • AI-authored analysis, not human-verified.

193,505 candidate pairs have viral-only NR evidence; 96,256 are absent from the NT20 baseline retained set. Requiring agreement between the viral-reference and NR species, and at least three common sequence-similarity clusters, yields 66 additional sample/species calls. Four are human-annotated threshold crossings, all from one or two baseline supports. None of those four starts from zero.

These are experimental sequence-evidence calls. The comparison applies common sequence-similarity grouping, but does not reproduce the production alignment-coordinate duplicate stage. It is not a completed production end-to-end pipeline run, an infection diagnosis, or a known-truth sensitivity measurement.

What was searched

The frozen 200-sample subset was screened through the protein detection path against translated viral references, then its candidates were checked against the broad NR protein database. This NR experiment changes the reference used for validation. The separate core_nt experiment keeps the original nucleotide reference and tests translated searches against it. The native comparisons include NT24, NT20 and the upstream-only union; the primary figures here use NT20.

The 200-sample protein candidate set contains 378,939 distinct complete trimmed sequence pairs, representing 447,209 original pairs. NR was searched as a larger pooled batch of 817,744 query mates including other frozen experiment panels. DIAMOND 2.1.17 used sensitive mode, 32 threads, block size 2, four index chunks, minimum ORF 1, genetic code 1, E-value 1e-5, 30% query coverage and top 10%. Classification requires at least 25 amino acids and uses taxa within 90% of the best eligible score. Each read keeps its own evidence.

Read gains and species resolution

Of the 96,256 additional viral-only exact pairs, 77,018 have no original member retained by the NT20 initial screen. 92,592 additional pairs have viral-only evidence on both mates. Across all 193,505 viral-only pairs, 46,714 resolve to a species in NR and 28,011 agree with the viral-reference species. Among the 96,256 additions, 70,662 have an NR assignment above species; 8,430 agree at species; 14,245 disagree at species; and 2,919 have an unresolved relationship between the two references.

Both-mate robustness check

Keeping the original common similarity groups fixed, requiring viral-only NR evidence on both mates reduces concordant protein-only gains from 66 to 53, gains from zero baseline support from 27 to 21, and human-annotated gains from four to two. Requiring both mates to align in the viral-reference search as well gives 52 gains, still 21 from zero and two human-annotated. Every original support count was reconstructed exactly from the saved cluster memberships before applying these two restrictions.

Matched protein-only panel

A frozen diagnostic panel contains one protein-only candidate pair per sample, absent from both NT24 and NT20, plus 32 shared controls. Among the 200 additions, megablast has viral-only evidence for 73, sensitive blastn for 132 and NR for 129. All 59 megablast-unaligned pairs rescued as viral-only by sensitive blastn also have viral-only NR evidence. Three pairs have viral-only NR evidence but competing nonviral evidence under blastn: SRR33038019_p1346, SRR38294105_p1807 and SRR38294706_p408. These disagreements require inspection; NR is not a truth label. The panel is sample-balanced and outcome-selected, not a read-weighted prevalence estimate.

Competing matches and comparison limits

129,099 candidate pairs have viral plus artificial-sequence competition only. 50,378 have some cellular competition. The latter categories are descriptive and can overlap other categories; artificial sequences must not be described as cellular matches. Primary viral-only/mixed classifications were not changed to improve the counts.

The baseline reads have not all been subjected to the identical NR-all-candidates requirement. Therefore the 156,320 baseline pairs missing from the NR-viral-only protein set are not a measured sensitivity loss. Species assignments, database composition, candidate generation and validation requirements differ. Taxonomy names and human annotations come from the frozen inputs and do not establish infection.

No larger default-TBLASTX panel is justified by this pilot; it will not be scaled. A separately bounded faster translated-query pilot uses the same 43 queries and unchanged core_nt.

NR comprises 1.172 billion sequences and 441.88 billion amino acids; its index occupies 918.57 GB. The raw output contains 2,392,256,108 alignment rows and 153.05 GB. core_nt contains 131,855,990 sequences and 1.089 trillion nucleotides; its frozen files occupy 318.55 GB. Search allocations exclude reference build/staging and idle time; those belong in the separate lifetime spend ledger. Commands and timing details are preserved in the search protocol files and completed-search-cost-comparison.json.

Verification and evidence

The original full-file Python aggregation and optimized query-call aggregation produce byte-identical classifications for all 406,173 pooled candidate pairs: SHA-256 d41350a484c89c566763df6704371079c7091821b935780bd0aadbeb5ba69cdc. The raw NR output and compact query evidence were downloaded from private S3 and independently hashed. The optimized representation does not replace the preserved raw alignments.
