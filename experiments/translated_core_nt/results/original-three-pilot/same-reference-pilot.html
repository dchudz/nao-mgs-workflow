Sequence checks

Fifteen changed mates with retained strict TBLASTX hits were checked against extracted original reference DNA. Eight had synonymous codon differences in the selected highest-scoring alignment. This verifies observed coding differences; it does not prove that any assignment is correct or that a synonymous change caused a nucleotide-search miss.

The two strict-hit losses are 68 nt mates, allowing at most 22 amino acids per full frame. Their best returned TBLASTX hits align 21 amino acids with E=0.002. Sensitive BLASTN instead finds a perfect 64 nt alignment with E=2.57×10⁻²². The two mate sequences are reverse complements, so these are not two independent examples.

Limits of these measurements

The 250-subject reporting cap was reached for 38 of 43 TBLASTX queries and 27 of 43 sensitive BLASTN queries. Omitted competing references can affect LCA resolution. This pilot does not establish complete competitor enumeration, known-truth accuracy, infection, or population sensitivity. NR cost-per-query comparison remains pending its completed search.

Translated alignment lengths and identities use amino acids; nucleotide methods use bases. The characterized translated coverage filter uses peptide-frame length, and raw TBLASTX coverage fields can differ from nucleotide span. Both frame and nucleotide coverage are preserved in the alignment tables. Standard genetic code 1 was used.
